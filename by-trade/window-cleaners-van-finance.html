Van Finance for Window Cleaners

Whether you're fitting out your first van with a water-fed pole system or upgrading to a bigger tank, your finance should match how your round actually pays you.

Why Window Cleaners Need the Right Van

Most window cleaners spend the day doing multiple short drops on a fixed route — pull up, run the pole up to gutter height, move on a few doors down, repeat. That stop-start pattern means access and manoeuvrability often matter as much as load space: many manage fine with a small panel van, while a bigger round with a full-size tank needs more payload and proper racking for poles and hose.

The tank is the detail that catches people out. A water-fed pole system needs a reservoir, and a full tank can add several hundred kilograms once hose reels and poles are loaded too — eating into your payload allowance fast. A small van (Combo/Berlingo-class) usually suits a solo round with a modest tank; a mid-size van (Transit Custom/Vivaro-class) gives more room for a bigger tank, ladders and stock.

Income tends to run on a cycle — four, six or eight-weekly visits to the same customers — so many window cleaners have a genuinely predictable weekly or monthly income even in their first year of trading. That recurring pattern can be easier to evidence for a finance application than lumpy, one-off invoicing, even for a newly self-employed sole trader.

Financing Considerations for Window Cleaners

A lot of window cleaners buying their first van are newly self-employed, often without several years of business accounts behind them. That doesn't rule out finance, but it does shape what you'll be asked for — expect weight put on bank statements, personal credit history and evidence of the round (regular customers, invoices, a rough round list) rather than formal accounts. Being upfront about how new the business is, and showing the round's recurring pattern, tends to help more than it hurts.

Because the van carries a loaded tank most days, check a used van's history and mileage carefully before you commit finance to it — heavy daily loads and short stop-start trips tend to add wear faster than typical commuting use. Hire purchase (HP) is the structure most window cleaners end up with, since this is a working tool they intend to keep and eventually own outright, rather than a vehicle handed back after a fixed term.

Before applying, it's worth running the numbers through the HP van finance calculator to see how a given van's price and term translate into a monthly payment, and the affordability calculator to check that payment against what the round actually brings in — particularly useful if you're basing income on a partial first year of trading.

Lenders Worth Comparing

  • Moneybarn — known as a specialist finance provider that considers applicants with limited or less-than-perfect credit history, worth a look for a first-time sole trader who hasn't built up much of a credit footprint yet.
  • Zuto — a broker that compares multiple lenders in one search, useful for weighing up options quickly on a smaller, lower-cost van suited to a modest round.

FAQ

Q: I've only been trading as a window cleaner for a few months — can I still get van finance?

A: Possibly. Lenders look at more than just how long you've been trading — your personal credit history, bank statements and evidence of a genuine, recurring round (repeat customers, regular invoices) can all help build the picture, even without several years of company accounts behind you.

Q: Will a water tank and pole system affect what van I can get finance for?

A: It can. A full tank adds real weight, so check a van's payload rating — not just its overall size — against your kit before financing it. A van that looks big enough on paper can still be over its legal carrying limit once the tank, hose reels and poles are loaded.

Q: Should I choose HP or PCP for a window cleaning van?

A: Most window cleaners go with hire purchase, since a work van doing daily rounds is a tool they're likely to keep and want to own outright at the end of the agreement, rather than hand back. PCP can suit if you prefer lower payments and like to change vehicles more often, but check any mileage and condition terms carefully given the wear a daily round puts on a van.
