Van Finance for Mobile Caterers and Street Food Traders

A street food van is really two purchases in one — the vehicle and the kitchen fitted inside it — so the finance needs to reflect both.

Why Mobile Caterers Need the Right Van

Street food and mobile catering is built around trading from a pitch — a festival field, a market square, a business park at lunchtime — with a full kitchen running inside the vehicle for hours at a stretch. That means the van isn't just transport; it's the kitchen, so head height to stand and cook, ventilation for extraction, and enough power capacity to run fryers, griddles and refrigeration all shape which van makes sense before a single conversion panel goes in.

Most mobile caterers end up with a long-wheelbase, high-roof panel van (Transit, Sprinter and Crafter-class vehicles are common), or a trailer-style horsebox conversion if they'd rather keep the kitchen separate from the tow vehicle. Which one suits depends on the menu — a burger van with a couple of griddles needs less power and space than a full fryer or pizza oven set-up — and on where you're trading, since narrower pitches and tighter festival access roads favour a more manoeuvrable vehicle over the biggest box available.

Income for most street food traders is genuinely seasonal and event-led: festivals and outdoor markets through spring and summer, a busier run-up to Christmas, and often a noticeably quieter winter. That's a different pattern to a trade with a steady weekly round, and it matters for how affordability gets assessed — rather than judging a single month's takings, it's usually more useful to show a full season (or more than one) of income, plus any bookings or deposits already lined up, so the picture reflects the whole year rather than just its busiest or quietest slice.

Financing Considerations for Mobile Caterers

A big part of a catering van's value sits in the fit-out, not the base vehicle — fryers, griddles, extraction, refrigeration, gas systems and a serving hatch can add up to as much as the van itself, sometimes more. That makes documentation genuinely useful: invoices for the conversion work, any weight and plating changes, and certification for gas or electrical installations all help a lender understand exactly what they're financing. It's a similar principle to financing a converted campervan — a well-documented, professionally fitted conversion is generally easier to value than one with no paper trail, even if two vehicles look similar from the outside.

Running a food business also means registering with your local council's environmental health team and holding a food hygiene rating, and any gas-powered equipment on board should be signed off by a Gas Safe registered engineer. None of this is finance paperwork as such, but keeping it organised alongside your conversion invoices tends to help — it's evidence of a genuine, compliant business rather than a part-built project, and exactly the kind of thing worth having to hand if a lender or broker asks questions about the vehicle.

FAQ

Q: I already have a horsebox/van and need finance for the conversion — is that different from financing the base van?

A: It can be. Some finance is arranged against the finished, converted vehicle (base van plus fit-out) and some purely against the base van before conversion — worth being clear with any lender or broker about which you need, since a professionally fitted-out catering van with proper documentation (invoices, certification) is generally easier to value than a part-finished project.

Q: My income is heavily seasonal — festivals and markets in summer, much quieter in winter. Does that hurt my chances?

A: It shouldn't rule you out, but it does change what's useful to show. A full season (or more than one) of takings, plus any bookings or deposits already lined up, gives a fuller picture than a single quiet month — and it's worth using an affordability calculator to check a payment holds up across your leanest months, not just your busiest ones.

Q: Do I need food hygiene or gas safety paperwork sorted before I apply for van finance?

A: It's not usually a strict requirement to apply, but having your food business registration, food hygiene rating and, if you're running gas equipment, Gas Safe certification in order helps show you're running a genuine, compliant operation — and it's exactly the kind of paperwork worth keeping alongside your conversion invoices for when a lender asks.
