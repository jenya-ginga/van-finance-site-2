Nissan Van Finance

Comparing finance for a Nissan van, whether that's the compact NV200/Townstar, the mid-size NV300 or the large NV400, works the same way whether you buy through a dealer or arrange it independently.

About Nissan's van range

Nissan's UK van range covers three main sizes: the NV200, sold alongside the newer Townstar as the small van in the line-up, the NV300 as the mid-size panel van, and the NV400 as the large van and chassis cab. Between them they cover much the same broad use cases as other manufacturers' ranges, from local trades and small business delivery up to bulkier loads and conversions.

Nissan holds a smaller share of the UK van market than some of the mainstream volume leaders, but it has a long-standing presence in the commercial vehicle space and was an early mover on electric vans here, with the e-NV200 among the first fully electric vans widely available in the UK. That early positioning is still part of how the brand is generally regarded in the van market, alongside its standard diesel and petrol range.

Because Nissan sells fewer vans overall than some competitors, the used market for NV200, NV300 and NV400 models tends to be somewhat smaller than for higher-volume rivals, though examples are still readily available through dealers and independent used van sellers. With a smaller pool to choose from, it can be worth casting a slightly wider net on location and being thorough about checking service history and condition before committing to a particular van.

Financing a Nissan van

As with any manufacturer, Nissan dealers typically offer their own finance when you buy new or used through them, and that's a legitimate route worth considering — but it isn't the only one. Comparing that offer against other lenders independently is a sensible check before signing anything.

Regardless of which route you take, the same factors are worth comparing: total cost over the term, deposit level, monthly payment against your actual budget, term length, and the conditions attached to early settlement or handing the van back. Running your numbers through the HP van finance calculator and an affordability calculator first gives you a realistic starting point before you weigh up what a dealer or any lender is offering.

Given Nissan's smaller share of the UK van market, it can be worth casting a slightly wider net across lenders too, rather than relying on a single option. Zuto and Moneybarn are both established names worth including in a comparison; Oodle Finance, Blue Motor Finance and Startline Motor Finance also sit on the panel we compare and are worth checking, since each lender's focus differs and comparing more than one is generally the best way to see what's actually available to you.

FAQ

Q: Can I finance a used Nissan NV200, NV300 or NV400 through an independent comparison site rather than a dealer?

A: Yes — independent comparison looks across a panel of lenders rather than a single dealer's finance offer, so it can be a useful way to check you're seeing a broader range of options.

Q: Is an electric Nissan van, such as the e-NV200, financed differently to a diesel model?

A: The process itself is generally similar, though what you can borrow and the monthly cost will reflect the vehicle's price and how a lender values it, so it's worth comparing electric and diesel options separately rather than assuming they'll come out the same.

Q: Are Nissan vans harder to finance because the brand has a smaller share of the UK van market?

A: Not necessarily — lenders finance a wide range of makes and models, but with fewer Nissan vans in circulation it may be worth widening your search across dealers and comparing more than one lender to see what's on offer.
