Vauxhall Van Finance

Whether you're after a compact Combo or a workhorse Movano, comparing finance from several lenders — rather than settling for the first offer at the dealership — is the simplest way to make sure a Vauxhall van fits your monthly budget.

About Vauxhall's van range

Vauxhall's current UK van line-up covers three size classes: the Combo is the small van, aimed at tradespeople and last-mile delivery work who need something easy to park and cheap to run; the Vivaro sits in the middle, a common choice for builders, couriers and small fleets that need more load space without stepping up to a large panel van; and the Movano is the large van, generally chosen for bulkier loads, chassis cab conversions and bigger operators.

Vauxhall has a long-standing presence in UK fleet and trade sales, and the current generation of all three vans is built on shared platforms with Stellantis stablemates — the Combo mirrors the Peugeot Partner and Citroën Berlingo, the Vivaro mirrors the Peugeot Expert and Citroën Dispatch, and the Movano mirrors the Peugeot Boxer and Citroën Relay. In practice, very similar vans are sold under several badges, so it's worth keeping an open mind on nameplate if a particular size and spec of Stellantis van comes up in your search.

Because Vauxhall vans have been a fleet mainstay for years, the used market tends to be reasonably well stocked across all three sizes, with plenty of ex-fleet and ex-lease examples reaching dealer forecourts at varying mileages and specifications. As with any used commercial vehicle, service history and how hard a van has worked matter more than badge alone, so it's worth checking those details regardless of which model you're considering.

Financing a Vauxhall van

Vauxhall dealers and the manufacturer's own finance arm are one way to fund a van purchase, and dealer finance can be worth a look, particularly if a specific promotional offer is running. But it's only one option — independent comparison across other lenders often turns up a structure that suits your circumstances better, especially if you're self-employed, a sole trader, or buying through a limited company.

Whichever route you take, compare the same things: the total amount repayable over the full term (not just the monthly figure), the deposit required, the length of the agreement, any mileage or usage conditions attached to the deal, and whether the finance product suits how you intend to use the van. Running the numbers through our HP van finance calculator and our affordability calculator before you commit gives you a clearer picture of what a deal will actually cost and whether the repayments sit comfortably alongside your other outgoings.

On the lender side, it can be worth getting quotes from a couple of our panel firms specifically because they focus on vehicle and van finance rather than general consumer credit. Moneybarn and Startline Motor Finance are both geared toward vehicle finance across a range of credit backgrounds, which can make them a useful starting point if a mainstream lender or dealer finance package hasn't offered terms you're happy with — though acceptance and terms always depend on your own circumstances and the lender's own assessment.

FAQ

Q: Can I get finance on a used Vauxhall van?

A: Yes — most of our panel lenders finance used vans as well as new ones, though the term length and deposit expected can vary depending on the vehicle's age and mileage.

Q: Is Vauxhall dealer finance better than comparing independently?

A: Not necessarily — dealer finance is convenient and sometimes comes with a manufacturer promotion, but it's still worth comparing it against other lenders to check you're getting a deal that suits your situation.

Q: Does it matter that the Vivaro shares its platform with other Stellantis vans?

A: Not for finance purposes — lenders assess the specific vehicle, its value and your application, so a shared platform doesn't change how the finance itself works, though it may widen your options if you're comparing similar-sized vans from sister brands.
