Volkswagen Van Finance

Compare finance for the Volkswagen Transporter, Crafter and Caddy, and see how independent comparison stacks up against dealer finance across VW's van range.

About Volkswagen's van range

Volkswagen's UK van line-up covers three main models. The Transporter is the mid-size van the brand is best known for, popular both as a workhorse and as a base for campervan and leisure conversions. The Crafter is VW's large van, competing with vehicles like the Ford Transit and Mercedes-Benz Sprinter for fleet and heavy-duty use. The Caddy is the small van, aimed at buyers who need car-like running costs with enough load space for tools or light deliveries.

The Transporter in particular has a strong general reputation in the used market for holding its value well compared with many rivals, which is part of why it's such a popular base for campervan conversions — buyers are often willing to pay more for a good used Transporter because resale values tend to stay relatively firm.

Because of that popularity, the used Volkswagen van market is wide and varied, ranging from high-mileage ex-fleet Transporters and Crafters to sought-after low-mileage examples and factory or aftermarket campervan conversions. That variety means prices can differ significantly for what looks like a similar spec, so it's worth comparing like-for-like before arranging finance.

Financing a Volkswagen van

Volkswagen dealers may offer their own finance arrangement when you buy through them, which is worth considering alongside independent comparison sites like this one that let you weigh that offer against other lenders rather than accepting the first quote you're given.

Regardless of route, focus on comparing the representative APR, the deposit required and the term length, since these determine your monthly payments and the total cost of the finance. Our HP van finance calculator can help you see how the numbers change with a different deposit or term, and our affordability calculator can help you sense-check the monthly repayment against your budget. If you're financing a used Transporter that's been converted for leisure use, it's also worth checking whether the lender treats it as a standard van or as a specialist/leisure vehicle, since this can affect which products are offered.

Given the Transporter's strong resale reputation and the wide spread of buyers — from tradespeople to campervan owners — it's worth comparing lenders that can accommodate both standard commercial finance and more personal, leisure-focused borrowing. Oodle Finance operates a largely online application process, which can suit buyers who want a quick, straightforward way to apply, while Zuto's broker model gives access to a panel of lenders, useful when comparing options for a popular, high-demand model like the Transporter.

FAQ

Q: Why do Volkswagen Transporters tend to hold their value?

A: The Transporter has a general reputation in the used market for holding its value relatively well compared with some rivals, partly due to steady demand from both trade and leisure/campervan buyers — though individual values still depend on age, mileage and condition.

Q: Can I finance a Volkswagen Transporter I plan to convert into a campervan?

A: In many cases yes, though it's worth checking with the lender whether they finance it as a standard van, and separately confirming how any future conversion work might affect insurance and warranty.

Q: Is it worth comparing finance for a Volkswagen Caddy the same way as a Transporter or Crafter?

A: Yes — the same core comparison points, representative APR, deposit and term, apply regardless of which VW van model you're financing.
