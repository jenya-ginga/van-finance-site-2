Citroën Van Finance

If you're comparing finance for a Citroën van, from a compact Berlingo to a long-wheelbase Relay, here's what to look at across the range and the practical points to check before you commit to a deal.

About Citroën's van range

Citroën's current UK van line-up runs from the Berlingo, a small van aimed at tradespeople and local delivery use, through the mid-size Dispatch, up to the Relay, a large panel van and chassis cab platform used for everything from parcel delivery to motorhome conversions. All three are also sold in car-derived and passenger-carrying versions, but the panel van variants are the ones most commonly seen in UK commercial fleets and among self-employed buyers.

Citroën is part of the Stellantis group, and its vans share engineering and, in many cases, production lines with sister models from Peugeot (Partner, Expert, Boxer) and Vauxhall (Combo, Vivaro, Movano). That platform-sharing means a Citroën van sits alongside several other well-established badges in the same broad market space, rather than standing apart from them, and Citroën has had a commercial vehicle presence in the UK for decades.

Because so many Berlingos, Dispatches and Relays have passed through UK fleets and small businesses over the years, the used market is generally broad — you'll find a wide range of ages, mileages and specifications through franchised dealers, independent used van dealers and private sellers. As with any used commercial vehicle, condition and history vary a lot more than with a new van, so it's worth having any used example checked over and its history verified before you arrange finance around it.

Financing a Citroën van

If you buy new or nearly-new through a Citroën dealer, dealer-arranged finance is usually offered as part of the sale — but that's one route among several, not the only way to finance the van. Comparing independently, across more than one lender, is a straightforward way to check whether a dealer's offer is actually competitive for your circumstances.

Whichever route you take, the things worth comparing stay the same: the total amount you'll repay over the full term (not just the monthly figure), the deposit required, how the term length affects your monthly payments, and what happens if you want to settle early or hand the van back. If the van is for business use, it's worth checking how that affects the finance arrangement too. Running the numbers through the HP van finance calculator and an affordability calculator before you speak to a dealer or lender is a useful way to see what's realistic for your budget in advance.

Lenders differ in how they assess applications and what they focus on, so it's generally worth comparing more than one rather than accepting the first offer you're given. Moneybarn and Zuto are both well-known names in vehicle finance and are commonly included in comparisons for that reason; Blue Motor Finance, Startline Motor Finance and Oodle Finance also sit on the panel we compare, and each may suit different circumstances, so it's worth seeing what each can offer before assuming any single lender is the right fit.

FAQ

Q: Can I get finance directly from a Citroën dealer for a Berlingo, Dispatch or Relay?

A: Yes, dealer-arranged finance is usually available when you buy through a Citroën dealer, but it's just one option — comparing it against other lenders can help you see whether it's the best deal for your situation.

Q: Is it harder to finance an older or high-mileage used Citroën van?

A: It can affect which lenders are willing to offer terms and what those terms look like, since older or higher-mileage vehicles are generally viewed differently to a new van. This varies by lender, so it's worth comparing rather than assuming.

Q: Do I need to be a registered business to finance a Citroën van?

A: Not necessarily — vans are financed by sole traders, limited companies and private individuals, and the right finance route depends on how you plan to use the van and your personal circumstances.
