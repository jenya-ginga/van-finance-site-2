Mercedes-Benz Van Finance

Compare finance for the Mercedes-Benz Sprinter, Vito and Citan, and see how independent comparison stacks up against dealer finance across this premium van range.

About Mercedes-Benz's van range

Mercedes-Benz's UK van range centres on three models. The Sprinter is the large van, widely used as a base for conversions such as Luton bodies, motorhomes and campervans thanks to its size and payload options. The Vito sits in the mid-size segment, often chosen by trades and courier-style businesses wanting a smaller footprint than the Sprinter. The Citan is the small van, built on the same underlying platform as the Renault Kangoo, giving Mercedes-Benz a presence at the smaller end of the market.

Mercedes-Benz vans are generally positioned as a premium option within the UK market, and buyers often choose the brand for a higher level of cabin comfort, specification and finish than some rival vans — particularly relevant if you spend long hours in the cab or are building a live-in conversion.

The used market for Mercedes-Benz vans, especially the Sprinter, is well established, with a wide range of ages, mileages and conversion types available — from bare panel vans to fully-fitted campervans and motorhomes built on a used Sprinter base. Because so many Sprinters are bought specifically as conversion platforms, it's worth checking a used van's history and any conversion work carefully alongside the finance itself.

Financing a Mercedes-Benz van

As with any manufacturer, Mercedes-Benz dealers may offer their own finance scheme when you buy new or used through them. That's one option to weigh up, but independent comparison — checking what's available through other lenders — can be worth doing before committing, especially given the higher price points typical of this range.

Whatever route you take, compare the same core figures: the representative APR, the deposit needed, and the term length, since even small differences in these terms can add up to a meaningful difference over the life of the agreement on a higher-value van. Our HP van finance calculator can help you model different deposit and term combinations, and our affordability calculator can help you check what monthly payment is realistic. If you're financing a converted vehicle, such as a motorhome built on a Sprinter base, check whether the lender treats it as a standard van or as a specialist/leisure vehicle, as this can affect the options available.

Given the higher values typically involved, it's worth comparing lenders with experience across a range of vehicle values and circumstances. Blue Motor Finance and Startline Motor Finance both operate as vehicle finance specialists working through dealers and brokers, which can suit buyers looking at higher-value used vans or those with less conventional circumstances, such as buying a converted vehicle rather than a standard panel van.

FAQ

Q: Can I get finance on a Mercedes-Benz Sprinter that's been converted into a campervan or motorhome?

A: Often, yes, but it's worth confirming with the lender whether they finance the vehicle as a standard van or as a leisure/converted vehicle, as this can affect which lenders and products are available.

Q: Is Mercedes-Benz van finance more expensive than other brands?

A: Mercedes-Benz vans typically have a higher purchase price than some mass-market rivals, which generally means a larger loan amount — the actual cost of finance still depends on the deposit, term and rate agreed rather than the badge alone.

Q: Should I use Mercedes-Benz's own finance or compare elsewhere first?

A: Dealer finance is worth considering, but comparing the representative APR, deposit and term against other lenders first can help you check whether it's competitive before you commit.

  • Blue Motor Finance — a vehicle finance specialist that may suit buyers considering higher-value used Mercedes-Benz vans.
  • Startline Motor Finance — geared toward specialist and non-standard finance situations, which can include converted or leisure-use vehicles.
