Panel Van Finance

The complete guide to financing a standard enclosed panel van — the most common commercial vehicle on UK roads, and generally the easiest to get competitively priced finance on.

What is a panel van used for?

The panel van is what most people picture when they think of "a van": the everyday working vehicle for electricians, plumbers, builders, couriers, delivery drivers and small businesses of every kind. It's bought in higher volumes than any other commercial vehicle type covered on this site, by the broadest possible range of trades and business sizes.

Panel vans come in a wide spread of sizes — short wheelbase (SWB) or long wheelbase (LWB), and low, medium or high roof — with most models sitting broadly in the 2.8 to 3.5 tonne gross vehicle weight range that a standard category B car licence can cover. Common models on the UK market include:

  • Ford Transit and Transit Custom
  • Mercedes-Benz Sprinter and Vito
  • Volkswagen Transporter and Crafter
  • Vauxhall Vivaro and Movano
  • Renault Trafic and Master, Peugeot Boxer/Expert, Citroën Relay/Dispatch

Payload varies hugely by size — a small van might carry a few hundred kilograms, while a large LWB high-roof van can carry over a tonne — so matching payload to your actual workload matters as much as price. Because panel vans are bought and sold in such high numbers, both new and used prices span a wide range, and the sheer volume in the used market generally means more choice at every price point than for a specialist or electric van.

Financing considerations for panel van

High sales volumes mean lenders have deep historical data on resale values, reliability and running costs for panel vans, which generally translates into more lenders competing for this type of business. For many buyers, that means the broadest lender choice and the most competitive representative APRs of any vehicle type on this site — comparing more than one quote is worthwhile precisely because there's more genuine competition to draw on.

If you're VAT-registered and buying mainly for business use, panel vans are typically treated by HMRC as commercial vehicles, meaning VAT on the purchase price may be reclaimable — unlike many cars. This is one reason panel van purchases are often financed through a business-facing agreement rather than a personal consumer credit route; confirm your own position with your accountant rather than assuming it applies.

Because most panel van buyers keep the vehicle as a long-term working tool rather than planning to hand it back at a set mileage, Hire Purchase (HP) — working toward outright ownership — tends to be the more common route than PCP, though PCP is still offered by some lenders. Use the HP van finance calculator to see how deposit, term and representative APR affect your monthly payment before you apply, and the affordability calculator to check it sits comfortably alongside your other business costs. As with any van finance, the exact rate offered depends on your circumstances and the lender's assessment.

FAQ

Q: What's the difference between financing a small panel van and a large one?

A: The core process — HP or PCP, credit check, deposit, term — is the same regardless of size, but a larger van generally means a bigger loan amount and higher monthly payment, and the lender will check the price reflects a realistic value for that size and spec.

Q: Can I finance a panel van through my business rather than personally?

A: Yes — many panel van purchases are financed as a business agreement, whether you trade as a sole trader, partnership or limited company. This is one reason panel van finance is often structured differently to personal car finance; ask the lender about the right agreement type for how you trade.

Q: Is HP or PCP better for a panel van I'll use every day for work?

A: If you're keeping the van long-term as a working tool rather than replacing it every few years, HP is usually the more straightforward fit than PCP, since there's no mileage restriction or return condition to manage — though it's worth comparing both using a calculator before deciding.
