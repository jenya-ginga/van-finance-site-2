Electric Van Finance

A practical guide to financing a battery-electric van in the UK, from range and payload trade-offs to how residual values affect your monthly payments.

What is an electric van used for?

Electric vans are most common among businesses doing urban and short-haul work: last-mile couriers, tradespeople based in or around towns and cities, and fleet operators with regular routes they can plan charging around. A growing number of self-employed drivers also choose electric to avoid ULEZ, Clean Air Zone and congestion charges in areas where those apply, alongside lower running costs on electricity versus diesel.

The UK market now includes several mainstream options, including:

  • Ford E-Transit
  • Mercedes-Benz eSprinter
  • Vauxhall Vivaro Electric (and its Stellantis siblings, such as the Peugeot e-Expert and Citroën ë-Dispatch)
  • Renault Master E-Tech
  • Volkswagen ID. Buzz Cargo and e-Transporter

The trade-off to understand before you buy is range versus payload: battery packs add weight, which typically reduces how much an electric van can carry compared with the equivalent diesel model, and real-world range usually falls short of the manufacturer's quoted figure once you load the van and factor in weather and driving style. New electric vans still carry a noticeable price premium over diesel equivalents, and because the used electric van market is younger and smaller, used choice and pricing are less established than for diesel vans — though both are growing.

Financing considerations for electric vans

A higher purchase price generally means a bigger loan amount, a bigger deposit, or both, so it's worth using the HP van finance calculator to model different deposit and term combinations before you commit, and the affordability calculator to check the monthly payment sits comfortably alongside your other business costs.

Residual values are harder to pin down for electric vans than for an established diesel model, simply because the used market and battery technology are both still evolving. This matters most on PCP agreements, where the final/minimum guaranteed value is central to how the monthly payment is worked out — if that projected value turns out to be optimistic or conservative, it changes the shape of the deal. It's worth comparing HP against PCP for this reason, rather than assuming the structure that suits a diesel van will suit an electric one in the same way.

On grants and tax: the government has previously run a Plug-in Van Grant reducing the price of new eligible electric vans, but the scheme's scope and grant amounts have been scaled back over recent years and, by 2026, no longer apply to most new vans — check current GOV.UK guidance before assuming a grant will reduce what you're financing. Separately, vans used privately are normally taxed via a flat-rate benefit-in-kind charge rather than the CO2-based system used for cars, and this charge has at times been reduced for zero-emission vans — again, check current HMRC guidance, since tax treatment changes. As with any van finance, the exact rate and terms you're offered depend on your circumstances and the lender's assessment.

FAQ

Q: Can I get finance on a used electric van, or only new?

A: Yes — HP and PCP agreements are available on used electric vans as well as new ones, though the range of used electric vans on the market is currently smaller than for diesel vans, and choice and pricing vary by lender.

Q: Does an electric van's range affect what I can borrow?

A: Lenders assess electric vans like any other vehicle asset, but because real-world range and resale values are less established than for diesel vans, this can influence a lender's view of the vehicle's future value — which in turn can affect the terms or PCP balloon amount offered.

Q: Is the plug-in van grant still available?

A: The plug-in van grant has been reduced and restricted over time and, for most new vans, no longer applies — check current GOV.UK guidance before assuming a grant will reduce the price you're financing.
