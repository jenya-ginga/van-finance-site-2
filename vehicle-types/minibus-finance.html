Minibus Finance

What you need to know before financing a 9-17 seat minibus, from licence categories to the permits and insurance costs that set minibuses apart from a standard van.

What is a minibus used for?

Minibuses are bought mainly by community transport groups, schools and school-run providers, care homes and day centres, small tour operators and coach hire firms, and organisations such as churches or sports clubs that need to move groups of people rather than goods. Many buyers are not-for-profit or charitable organisations rather than typical sole traders, which affects how a purchase gets assessed and financed.

Licence category matters as soon as you go beyond 8 passenger seats. A standard category B car licence generally covers minibuses with up to 8 passenger seats; beyond that you typically need category D1, unless you passed your driving test before 1 January 1997 and hold grandfather rights allowing you to drive a minibus without it — this is worth confirming directly with DVLA rather than assuming either way. Common models on the UK market include:

  • Ford Transit minibus
  • Mercedes-Benz Sprinter minibus
  • Volkswagen Crafter minibus
  • Iveco Daily minibus
  • Peugeot Boxer minibus and LDV Maxus

New minibuses typically cost noticeably more than an equivalent-size panel van, because of the seating conversion, safety features and any accessibility equipment such as wheelchair lifts or tracked seating. Used minibus prices vary widely depending on age, mileage, seat configuration and what conversion work has been done.

Financing considerations for minibus

Because a minibus (vehicle plus conversion) usually costs more than a comparable panel van, loan sizes and monthly repayments tend to be higher — it's worth running the figures through the affordability calculator alongside your other running costs before you apply, rather than looking at the finance payment in isolation.

If the minibus will carry passengers for payment rather than on a private or wholly not-for-profit basis, operators may need a Section 19 (or Section 22) permit, or in some cases a full PSV/PCV operator's licence — this is a legal operating requirement separate from the finance agreement itself, but a lender may still ask about intended use, who will drive it, and whether it's for hire and reward, since this affects their view of the vehicle's use and risk.

Insurance is usually a bigger factor in total cost than it is for a standard panel van, because minibus cover includes passenger liability. Many community operators also put drivers through recognised training schemes (such as MiDAS) partly because insurers and permit-issuing bodies expect it, and this kind of operational detail can form part of how a lender assesses an application — particularly for charities or community groups whose borrowing profile looks different from a typical self-employed applicant.

FAQ

Q: Do I need a special driving licence to drive a minibus?

A: If the minibus has more than 8 passenger seats you'll usually need category D1 on your licence, unless you passed your test before 1 January 1997 and hold grandfather rights — check directly with DVLA for your specific situation.

Q: Can our community group get finance if we're not VAT-registered or a limited company?

A: Charities, community groups and small operators can apply for minibus finance, though the assessment may look at your legal structure and finances differently to a standard self-employed applicant — some lenders are more used to non-standard borrowing profiles than others.

Q: Does minibus finance cost more than van finance because of insurance?

A: Finance itself is priced on the vehicle and your credit profile, but minibus insurance — which covers passenger liability — is often the biggest additional running cost compared with a standard panel van, so budget for it separately from the monthly finance payment.
