Campervan Finance

Compare finance options for converted campervans, from compact VW-style conversions to larger panel-van builds, bought for weekends and holidays rather than work.

What is a campervan used for?

Campervans are almost always a private, leisure purchase rather than a business one. Typical buyers include couples and families wanting flexible UK or European trips, retirees looking for a slower way to travel, and festival or outdoor-activity regulars who want somewhere to sleep and cook without towing a caravan. It's a lifestyle vehicle first, and any practical carrying ability is a secondary bonus.

The entry point for many buyers is a Volkswagen Transporter or California-class conversion, with a pop-top roof, elevating bed and small kitchen unit. Larger conversions are built on base vans like the Ford Transit Custom, Volkswagen Crafter, Mercedes Sprinter or Renault Trafic, giving more headroom and living space and blurring the line with a small motorhome. Conversions can come from established specialist converters with a track record, or as one-off self-builds — the difference matters more here than with an unconverted van.

Prices span a very wide range, since a well-used self-converted panel van and a nearly-new professionally converted California sit at opposite ends of the market — treat any figure as illustrative rather than a rule. New, professionally converted campervans typically carry a substantial premium over the price of the bare base van. And condition of the conversion itself — damp, wiring, gas safety, the quality of the fit-out — often matters as much to value as the mileage on the odometer.

Financing considerations for campervans

Because campervans are usually a personal rather than a business purchase, financing typically runs through consumer credit routes — personal HP or a personal loan — assessed against your own income and outgoings rather than business trading figures. This is a different affordability conversation to a work van, and it's worth running your own numbers through an affordability calculator before you apply.

Conversion quality also affects lender appetite, not just resale value. Because part of what you're paying for is the conversion rather than just the base van, some lenders are more comfortable financing a vehicle with a clear build record — invoices, a conversion certificate, evidence of gas and electrical safety checks — than a self-build with little paperwork. If you're financing a DIY conversion, expect more questions from a lender and a smaller pool of lenders willing to consider it; a specialist route may suit better than a mainstream one.

Campervans also see seasonal demand. Interest and resale values tend to firm up heading into spring and summer and soften over winter, which can affect what you get back if you part-exchange or sell partway through an agreement. It's worth weighing this alongside ordinary mileage-and-condition depreciation when deciding how much to borrow and over what term.

FAQ

Q: Can I get finance for a self-converted or DIY campervan?

A: It's possible but generally harder than financing a professionally converted vehicle. Lenders tend to prefer conversions with clear documentation — build records, receipts, gas and electrical safety certificates — since these make the vehicle easier to value. A self-build with little paperwork may need a specialist lender, or could be declined by some.

Q: Does the quality of the conversion affect how much I can borrow?

A: Generally, yes. A campervan's value reflects both the base vehicle and the standard of the conversion, so a well-documented, professionally converted van is usually easier for a lender to value fairly than an unrecorded or lower-quality build.

Q: Is campervan finance the same as van finance for a business?

A: Not usually. Most campervans are financed as a private purchase through consumer credit — personal HP or a loan, assessed on your own income — rather than through business or commercial asset finance, even though the base vehicle may be a panel van model also sold for trade use.
