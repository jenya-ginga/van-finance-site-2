HPI & finance history check: what it actually covers

A five-minute check that can stop you buying a van that's stolen, written off, clocked, or still owned by a finance company.

What's included in a full check

Outstanding financeConfirms whether the van is still subject to an HP/PCP/lease agreement — buying it wouldn't give you legal ownership.
Write-off / insurance statusShows Cat A/B/S/N write-off history, which affects safety, insurability and resale value.
Stolen markerCross-checks the Police National Computer stolen vehicle database.
Mileage discrepancyCompares recorded MOT mileage readings to flag possible clocking.
Plate change historyShows if the registration plate has been changed, which can hide a vehicle's true history.
Import / export statusFlags whether the vehicle was imported, which can affect parts availability and specification.

Why this matters for finance specifically

If a van still has outstanding finance registered against it and you buy it from a private seller, you risk the original finance company repossessing the vehicle — even though you paid for it in good faith. This is the single most common reason to run a check before a private-sale purchase, and it's separate from (and just as important as) checking your own finance eligibility.

Free vs paid checks

You can get limited free information from the DVLA (tax and MOT status, for example) but a full multi-database history check — including outstanding finance and write-off status — is a paid service from providers such as HPI, Experian AutoCheck, or via most major insurers and the AA/RAC. Expect to pay roughly £10–£20 for a single check.

Recommended check order

  1. Run our free VIN format check first.
  2. Ask the seller for the V5C logbook and confirm the VIN matches the vehicle.
  3. Run a paid HPI/finance check using the VIN and registration.
  4. Only proceed with payment once outstanding finance shows clear.
My VanFinance does not run HPI checks directly on this page — this is an educational guide. Use an accredited provider for the paid check itself.
